Risk spine

Eight governors, composed

The risk spine is designed to be correct rather than profitable. Risk controls do not require a forward-looking edge to function; they require that they cannot be bypassed. Each governor independently emits a clamp, and a clamp book aggregates them deterministically.

The aggregation rule is the load-bearing design decision. Multipliers compose via min(), not by averaging, and the book fails closed — the most restrictive clamp wins. Averaging would let a permissive governor dilute a restrictive one, so a system that averages one "halt" against three "fine"s keeps trading. Taking the minimum means any single governor can stop the system on its own, and the console always names which one is binding.

Hazard
Shield

Blocks individual trades on hazard conditions before they can reach sizing. 146 catalogued micros.

Aggression
WRAB

Regime-adaptive aggression scaling. The largest module in the system at 229 catalogued micros.

Tiered cut
Drawdown circuit breaker

Steps exposure down in tiers as intraday drawdown deepens, rather than cutting in one jump.

Tail sizing
CVaR tail sizer

Sizes off conditional tail expectation rather than variance — it cares about the bad tail, not the average.

Overshoot
Equity bands & cool-off

Anti-overshoot bands stop equity-driven sizing running away from itself; a steady-state governor enforces cool-off after abnormal activity.

Re-entry
Recovery parachute

Staged, rate-limited re-entry after a drawdown event instead of an immediate return to full size.

Capital
Allocator

Distributes capital across the six sleeves under every constraint above. 190 catalogued micros.

Governance
Omega ops

Top-level operational circuit and credit budget — the governor that can stand the whole system down.

"""Profit Pilot — Risk Clamp Book.

Multiple modules emit `risk.clamp` events (drawdown breaker, equity bands,
loss-day fingerprint, recovery parachute, etc.).

Execution needs a single coherent answer to questions like:
- "Am I allowed to open new positions right now?"
- "What risk multiplier applies to this sleeve/symbol?"
- "Is the system in exit-only mode?"

Design principles
-----------------
- *Fail closed*: the most restrictive clamp wins.
- Multipliers compose via `min()` (i.e., smallest multiplier dominates).
- Entry denial and exit-only flags follow highest-priority clamps.
- TTL is in bars and decays on `market.bar` ticks.
"""

Composition is by min(), not by product or average. Averaging permits a permissive governor to dilute a restrictive one: a system averaging one halt against three clears continues trading. Under a minimum, any single governor halts the system independently, and each governor added can only reduce exposure, never increase it. The distinction is between a risk layer that is structurally constrained and one that is behaviourally constrained.

THE EVENT LINEAGE IS THE WHOLE AUDITABILITY ARGUMENT. The console does not merely report that WRAB is clamping exposure to ×0.29 — it lists the 187 individual events that set that value, each reason-coded (catalyst_event_heat, poc_support_lower, poc_prefade_upper) and timestamped to the second. A risk control that can only tell you its output is a number you have to trust. One that can replay the events which produced it is a number you can check — and that is the difference between a system a firm could run and a system a person could only believe in.
