Risk spine

Eight governors, composed

This is the part of the system I would defend most confidently, because it is designed to be correct rather than profitable. Risk controls don't need a forward-looking edge in order to work — they need to be structurally impossible to bypass. Each governor independently emits a clamp, and a clamp book aggregates them deterministically.

The aggregation rule is the design decision worth defending. Multipliers compose via min(), not by averaging, and the book fails closed — the most restrictive clamp wins. Averaging would let a permissive governor dilute a restrictive one, so a system that averages one "halt" against three "fine"s keeps trading. Taking the minimum means any single governor can stop the system on its own, and the console always names which one is binding.

Hazard
Shield

Blocks individual trades on hazard conditions before they can reach sizing. 146 catalogued micros.

Aggression
WRAB

Regime-adaptive aggression scaling. The largest module in the system at 229 catalogued micros.

Tiered cut
Drawdown circuit breaker

Steps exposure down in tiers as intraday drawdown deepens, rather than cutting in one jump.

Tail sizing
CVaR tail sizer

Sizes off conditional tail expectation rather than variance — it cares about the bad tail, not the average.

Overshoot
Equity bands & cool-off

Anti-overshoot bands stop equity-driven sizing running away from itself; a steady-state governor enforces cool-off after abnormal activity.

Re-entry
Recovery parachute

Staged, rate-limited re-entry after a drawdown event instead of an immediate return to full size.

Capital
Allocator

Distributes capital across the six sleeves under every constraint above. 190 catalogued micros.

Governance
Omega ops

Top-level operational circuit and credit budget — the governor that can stand the whole system down.

"""Profit Pilot — Risk Clamp Book.

Multiple modules emit `risk.clamp` events (drawdown breaker, equity bands,
loss-day fingerprint, recovery parachute, etc.).

Execution needs a single coherent answer to questions like:
- "Am I allowed to open new positions right now?"
- "What risk multiplier applies to this sleeve/symbol?"
- "Is the system in exit-only mode?"

Design principles
-----------------
- *Fail closed*: the most restrictive clamp wins.
- Multipliers compose via `min()` (i.e., smallest multiplier dominates).
- Entry denial and exit-only flags follow highest-priority clamps.
- TTL is in bars and decays on `market.bar` ticks.
"""

The choice worth defending is composition by min() rather than by product or average. Averaging would let a permissive governor dilute a restrictive one — a system that averages one "halt" against three "fine"s keeps trading. Taking the minimum means any single governor can stop the system alone, and adding a ninth governor can only ever make the system safer, never riskier. That is the difference between a risk layer that is structurally safe and one that is merely behaviourally safe, and it is why the clamp book is one of the few places in this codebase where I would defend every line.
