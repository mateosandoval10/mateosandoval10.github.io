Profit Pilot · strategy layer

Six independent
sleeves.

Each sleeve is a separate signal producer with its own cadence, universe and risk budget. They are deliberately uncorrelated in mechanism, so that no single market regime can disable all of them at once — and they compete for capital through the allocator rather than being allotted it.

Why sleeves at all

Separation is a risk decision,
not an organisational one

A single strategy with many conditions inside it is one failure away from silence — and when it fails, there is no way to tell which condition broke. Splitting the book into sleeves buys three specific things: each one can be measured for its own expectancy, each can be throttled or halted independently by the risk spine, and each can be retired without touching the others. The cost is that every sleeve has to justify its capital against the rest, every day.

Signal contract
Every sleeve emits strategy.signal events onto the same bus. A sleeve does not size the trade, choose the venue, or decide whether it is allowed — it makes a claim, and the rest of the platform adjudicates
Capital competition
The allocator distributes capital across all six under the risk spine's constraints. A sleeve that stops earning its risk budget is throttled, and eventually refused promotion
Independent halt
Any governor can clamp a single sleeve without touching the others — clamp scope is GLOBAL, SLEEVE or SYMBOL
Cadence isolation
A sub-second sleeve and a multi-day sleeve share no timing assumptions, so neither can starve or overwhelm the other

What it trades
Multi-day equity positions — the primary book the rest of the platform is calibrated around
Cadence
Daily exposure logic with portfolio balancing; the slowest sleeve in the system
Design intent
Explicitly conservative alpha. The engine's own specification says it should survive anything and keep compounding, and that it defers aggressive behaviour to Moonshot and Day Trading
Why it is separate
It is the sleeve that must still be standing after a bad quarter. Mixing it with faster, convex strategies would let their drawdowns dictate its sizing
How it differs
Regime-aware and catalyst-aware, but not event-triggered like Inside Mode and not microstructure-driven like Day Trading. It reacts to the state of the market, not to individual prints
Capital competition
Holds the baseline allocation; the other sleeves bid for capital above it

Day Trading

Intraday · microstructure
v6.0
Engine version

Catalogued micros
18

What it trades
Same-session equity entries and exits, sized off intraday volatility
Cadence
The fastest sleeve — dedicated tick-horizon learners at 30 seconds, 1 minute and 5 minutes, combined by entropy-weighted fusion so the horizon with the clearest signal dominates
What makes it work
Queue-aware microstructure features built from trade, quote and order-book streams, plus a flow predictor on a 1–3 second horizon. It is the only sleeve that models the order book rather than the bar
Its own safety layer
A microstructure shock sentinel watches for spread blowout and depth collapse and auto-resets after 2–3 bars; a dynamic risk thermostat responds to intraday volatility clustering; a time-of-day heat map enforces what the spec calls no-EOD-binge discipline
Why it is separate
Sub-second and multi-day logic cannot share a risk model. A stop that is correct for a swing position is noise at a one-minute horizon, and the reverse
Capital competition
Highest turnover of any sleeve, so it is judged on risk-adjusted contribution rather than gross points — the same standard applied in the intraday futures research

Inside Mode

Event-driven · flow
v5.0
Engine version

Catalogued micros

What it trades
Positions formed from clustered insider, institutional and political flow signals around catalyst events
Cadence
Event-triggered rather than scheduled — it consumes catalyst.event messages alongside market bars
Mechanism
Builds an inside-flow cluster score per symbol and emits a signal only when the cluster is strong enough. A single filing is not a trade; a convergence of them might be
Attribution feedback
When a post-event move exceeds a threshold the sleeve emits a catalyst.attribution_request, so the Catalyst Map can learn the reliability of that event type. The sleeve feeds the system that scores it
Why it is separate
Its inputs are filings and disclosures, not prices. It goes quiet for long stretches, which would look like failure inside a price-driven sleeve
Capital competition
Bursty by nature — it draws capital when clusters form and returns it when they do not, which is why it sits behind the allocator rather than holding a standing allocation

Arbitrage

What it trades
Relationships rather than direction — ADR against ordinary listings, and dislocations around auctions and reopens
Cadence
Opportunistic, gated on a spread z-score reaching an extreme
Mechanism
Emits paired signals — two legs at once — and downstream components must treat them as a group via meta.arb_group. A half-filled pair is not a position, it is exposure
Execution requirements
Atomic tri-leg IOC/FAK execution, because legging into a relative-value trade at market is how the edge gets paid to somebody else. A borrow-aware short guard and a corporate-actions guard sit in front of it
Why it is separate
It is the only sleeve whose signal is inherently multi-symbol, and the only one whose execution failure mode is partial success. That demands its own contract
Capital competition
Shares a capital cycle with Core and Day Trading, so a widening spread can pull capacity away from directional books when the relative-value opportunity is better

What it trades
Small, asymmetric, catalyst-confirmed positions — deliberately low hit rate, deliberately capped loss
Cadence
Time-boxed. A convexity thesis that has not paid within its window is closed, not held in hope
Sizing philosophy
Sized so that being wrong repeatedly is survivable. This is the sleeve where position sizing does the work, not signal quality
Self-limiting behaviour
Auto-pause on cold streak and an adaptive equity-curve governor mean the sleeve stands itself down after a run of losses, rather than waiting for the risk spine to do it
Why it is separate
Its return distribution is the opposite shape to Core's. Averaged into a conservative book its variance would look like a defect; isolated, it is the point
Capital competition
Confidence-gated allocation — it draws meaningful capital only when a catalyst is confirmed, and skims profit off bursts rather than pyramiding into them

WHY THE BOOK IS SPLIT. Six sleeves means six independent answers to "is this still working?" — and six places the platform can intervene without touching anything else. A monolithic strategy gives you one answer and one lever. The risk spine is what makes the separation enforceable, and the module directory shows what each sleeve is actually built out of.
Current status

Profit Pilot is a working trading system, running continuously against a live brokerage API with real market data. It is in an independent testing stage ahead of public release — live capital is gated at the configuration layer by allow_live_trading: false while the five-layer test stack does its work.
