Profit Pilot · strategy layer

Six independent
sleeves.

Each sleeve is a separate signal producer with its own cadence, universe and risk budget. They are deliberately uncorrelated in mechanism, so that no single market regime can disable all of them at once — and they compete for capital through the allocator rather than being allotted it.

Why sleeves at all

Separation is a risk decision,
not an organisational one

A single strategy with many conditions is one failure from silence, and gives no way to tell which condition broke. Splitting the book buys three things: each sleeve is measured, throttled and retired on its own.

Signal contract
Every sleeve emits strategy.signal events onto the same bus. A sleeve does not size the trade, choose the venue, or decide whether it is allowed — it makes a claim, and the rest of the platform adjudicates
Capital competition
The allocator distributes capital across all six under the risk spine's constraints. A sleeve that stops earning its risk budget is throttled, and eventually refused promotion
Independent halt
Any governor can clamp a single sleeve without touching the others — clamp scope is GLOBAL, SLEEVE or SYMBOL
Cadence isolation
A sub-second sleeve and a multi-day sleeve share no timing assumptions, so neither can starve or overwhelm the other

WHY THE BOOK IS SPLIT. Six sleeves means six independent answers to "is this still working?" — and six places the platform can intervene without touching anything else. A monolithic strategy gives you one answer and one lever. The risk spine is what makes the separation enforceable, and the module directory shows what each sleeve is actually built out of.
Current status

Profit Pilot is my own trading system, built solo over more than a year and running continuously against a live brokerage API with real market data. It has been validated through tens of thousands of backtest runs, deterministic replay, out-of-sample gating, Monte Carlo scenarios and sustained forward testing in paper. It goes live shortly.
