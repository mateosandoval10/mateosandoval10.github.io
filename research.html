methodology.

A validation pipeline built to reject its own results. Four independent layers — a placebo null, bootstrap confidence intervals, a frozen-config out-of-sample protocol, and per-component audits. It has retired one deployed engine and returned a null on a configuration in the frozen committee.

Why this exists

Chase a big backtest,
then price what it was worth

Search a parameter grid wide enough and something in it will look excellent. That is not a finding; it is the maximum of a noisy sample. The problem was therefore measured directly before any result was relied on.

Protocol, run once and reported as-is: enumerate 60,480 mechanics configurations of the VWAP-fade family — z-threshold, z-window, stop, target style, breakeven arm, trades per day, entry window. Score every one on train (2024–2025) only. Then take those exact frozen configurations and run them on 2026, which the optimisation never saw. All runs use realistic fills.

THE RESULT. Across all 60,480 configurations the correlation between train net and test net is +0.087 — a big backtest number carries almost no information about what comes next. Broad train performance does carry a little signal (top-decile median test +374 pts against +184 for the bottom decile), but the extreme tail inverts: the top 100 configs — precisely the ones an optimiser would hand you — produced a median loss out of sample and lost money 79% of the time, while picking a config at random from the same family returned a median +265 pts. Selecting on the backtest maximum was worse than not selecting at all.

Everything below is built around that finding. The pipeline never reports a best cell as a result; it reports the behaviour of a region, and it tries to kill that region three different ways before believing it.

What is being tested

One small engine, one strict cost model

A single research engine, deliberately small, so the surface available for fitting stays small. Three strategy families are implemented — a session-VWAP z-score fade, an opening-range breakout, and an intraday EMA-cross trend follower — on Nasdaq-100 E-mini (NQ) futures bars at 30-minute and 1-hour resolution. The fade family is the one that survived far enough to be worth validating.

Layer one

Placebo testing

A p-value against a textbook null is worthless here, because the alternative hypothesis isn't "returns are non-zero" — it's "this strategy exploits real serial dependence in intraday price". So the null has to be a market with that dependence surgically removed and everything else intact.

How a placebo world is built

Permute bars within the session

Each bar is decomposed into its body, upper wick, lower wick, and open gap versus the prior close. Bar order is permuted within each ET session, and prices re-chained so every bar keeps its own shape and gap but occupies a random slot in the day.

What survives, what dies

Volatility kept, sequence destroyed

The daily bar count, the distribution of bar ranges, the session volatility profile and the overall drift are preserved. The intraday sequence a VWAP fade depends on is not. A real edge should collapse here; one fitting noise should not care.

oc  = C - O                      # body
ho  = H - max(O, C)              # upper wick
lo  = min(O, C) - L              # lower wick
gap = O - prev_close             # open gap vs prior close

for each ET session:
    perm = rng.permutation(bar_indices)
    for slot, src in zip(bar_indices, perm):
        o = new_close[slot-1] + gap[src]   # re-chain
        c = o + oc[src]
        h = max(o, c) + ho[src]
        l = min(o, c) - lo[src]

In every world — 150 placebos per timeframe, plus the real data — a fixed representative grid of 48 configurations is run (3 z-thresholds × 4 z-windows × 4 stop distances), and the median Sharpe across that grid is recorded. Using the median rather than the maximum is the entire point: the median asks whether a whole region of parameter space works, which is much harder for noise to fake.

SURVIVES. On both timeframes the real plateau-median Sharpe sits outside the placebo distribution's 95th percentile: 13 in 1,000 placebo worlds beat the real 30-minute result, 33 in 1,000 on the 1-hour. Structure the shuffle destroys is structure the strategy was using.
SELECTION BIAS, MEASURED DIRECTLY. The best-cell p-values (0.073 and 0.053) are markedly weaker than the median p-values (0.013 and 0.033). That is the signature of maximum-selection bias, measured directly: a shuffled world with no exploitable structure at all can still produce one impressive-looking cell out of 48. It is why this page reports plateau medians everywhere and never a headline best config.

Resolution limit: with 150 worlds the smallest p-value this test can resolve is 1/151 ≈ 0.007. These p-values sit near that floor and should be read as "unlikely under the null", not as precise probabilities.

Layer two

Bootstrap confidence intervals

Surviving the placebo says an effect is unlikely to be pure noise. It says nothing about the size of that effect or the confidence around it. For that: 10,000 resamples of the daily P&L series, with replacement, for each frozen configuration — producing a distribution of Sharpe, net result and drawdown rather than a single number. Probability of a losing month is estimated separately by resampling 21-day blocks.

Layer three

Frozen-config out-of-sample

The first two layers run on data already examined. Neither rules out that configuration choices were shaped by what was observed. Only withheld data does that, and only if the configuration is frozen first.

The sealed vault

On 2026-07-27, before any of the 2024 data had been examined, four configurations were written to a file with a stated protocol and a pre-registered success criterion. The 2024 slice of the hourly dataset — 5,920 bars — has never been backtested by any strategy in this project. The sweep code enforces that in software, not by discipline:

VAULT_CUT = "2025-01-01"          # nothing before this date is visible
d = d[d.et_date >= VAULT_CUT].reset_index(drop=True)   # seal the vault
Pre-registered criterion. Run each frozen config once on 2024, report as-is, no re-tuning. Success = committee-median 2024 Sharpe > 0 and committee-median net > 0 under realistic fills. Single-config misses do not fail the test; a negative committee median does.

Status: still sealed. The test has not been run. It is a one-shot instrument — running it converts the last genuinely clean sample in this project into in-sample data, and it is worth more unspent until the research it would validate is finished. That it remains unopened is itself part of the protocol; the criterion is published now precisely so the result cannot be reinterpreted afterwards.

The forward window that has been run

A separate, smaller check has been executed. The deployed CORE book — an AM engine plus the PM VWAP fade — was built and tuned on sessions through 2026-07-17. Nine further sessions were exported after that window closed and had never been seen by any fit. Frozen config, realistic fills, run once:

The model expected +19.1 points per session — +172 over nine sessions, with a 95% band of −728 … +1,072 at the book's ~150 points/session standard deviation. The realised −0.7 gives z = −0.38: inside the band, but only just informative.

What nine sessions can and cannot say. At 150 points of daily noise, a nine-session window cannot confirm or refute an edge of this size; the confidence band is roughly ten times wider than the effect being measured. This is a consistency check — evidence that nothing is catastrophically broken — and it is reported as one. A page presenting these nine sessions as validation would be misrepresenting its own statistics.
Layer four

Component audits

The last question is not whether the book works, but whether every part of it earns its place. The trap is a component that adds points while adding more risk than points — it improves the headline and degrades the portfolio. This audit asks for risk-adjusted contribution across every available session, split into the window the book was tuned on, a later test window, and the sessions exported after tuning ended.

RETIRED — the AM engine. Across every segment the AM engine adds net points (+183 over 125 sessions) while cutting the book's Sharpe from 2.16 to 1.38, and it carries a maximum drawdown of −1,696 points against the PM fade's −652. It was positive in only 3 of 7 months. Its correlation with the PM fade is +0.28, so it was not even providing diversification — it was providing variance. It had been kept because it added points. Points are the wrong objective; it was retired.

This layer is the clearest evidence that the process binds. It removed a working component from a deployed book, and the metric that had justified keeping it — total points — was the wrong objective.

Limitations

What this page is not

The list this work would be dishonest without. It is here as a section rather than a footnote because it carries as much information as the results do.

Known limitations
Research output · not a track record
Scope
One instrument, one market era. Everything here is NQ futures, 2024–2026. Nothing establishes that the effect exists on other contracts, or existed before this window.
Strongest test unspent
The vault test has not been run. The best evidence this project could produce is, by design, still sealed. Until it is opened, every result here is either in-sample or rests on a nine-session forward window.
Sample size
Small throughout. 56 to 214 trading days per config; nine sessions in the forward test; 125 in the component audit. The bootstrap intervals are wide because the evidence is thin — that width is honest, not a formatting choice.
Bootstrap assumption
Resampling daily P&L with replacement treats sessions as exchangeable. Volatility clusters, so this understates the probability of extended drawdowns. The intervals are, if anything, too narrow.
Placebo assumption
Within-session permutation removes intraday sequence but keeps the daily volatility profile, the bar-range distribution and the drift. A strategy exploiting any of those would survive the placebo without having a sequential edge.
Null resolution
150 worlds per timeframe gives a smallest resolvable p-value of ≈0.007. The reported p-values sit near that floor.
Fill optimism
Stops are assumed to fill at the stop price. Real gap and fast-market fills are worse, and the 1.5-point slippage assumption does not fully cover that tail.
Nature of the results
These are simulated results on historical bars under stated fill assumptions. No figure on this page is a realised trading return, and none should be read as a forecast.
Implementation
The Python was written with AI assistance. Protocols, choice of statistics and interpretation are the author's; the code was reviewed and run by the author. Stated rather than left to be inferred.
